1. Rhetorical Analysis Essay

Prompt: Analyze the rhetorical choices Gandhi makes in his 1930 letter to Viceroy Lord Irwin to present his case against Britain's colonial monopoly on salt.

The Crucible of Conscience: Rhetorical Strategies in Gandhi’s Letter

In his 1930 letter to Viceroy Lord Irwin, Mohandas Gandhi argues that British colonial rule operates as a devastating "curse" that systematically erodes India economically, politically, and morally. Gandhi constructs his argument by establishing a spiritual and moral framework to find shared ground with his recipient, utilizing conditional "if-then" structures to frame the impending conflict as a choice for Britain, and adopting a humble, service-oriented tone to solidify his selfless credibility...

Revision Deep Dive

Before Revision After Revision Rhetorical Commentary
The effect of this two-choice situation does not sound as strong as it should be, but it left the burden of responsibility solely on Lord Irwin. This binary framing strips the colonial administration of strategic ambiguity, placing the entire burden of choice and subsequent escalation squarely on Lord Irwin’s shoulders. The original draft misidentified Gandhi's restraint as weakness. The revision properly evaluates how his conditional logic serves to trap his opponent strategically.

2. Argument Essay

Prompt: Formulate an argument on whether universities should implement a temporary, mandatory phone ban for incoming freshmen during orientation.

Unplugging to Connect: The Case for a Temporary Orientation Phone Ban

Have you ever glanced at your screen-time report and felt an underlying sense of unease regarding the hours spent mindlessly scrolling? For many college students, that feeling is an inescapable daily reality. While some critics worry that a temporary phone ban might counterproductively cut students off from vital family lifelines, a brief two-day restriction at the absolute start of the college experience provides something far more invaluable: it systematically curbs unhealthy digital dependence and actively fosters deeper human connections...

Revision Deep Dive

Before Revision After Revision Rhetorical Commentary
Problems caused by phone could be demonstrated by a myriad of evidences... Without a break from constant digital distractions, students' academic careers could be derailed... The structural problems catalyzed by excessive phone usage are demonstrated by a wealth of empirical data... Without a deliberate break from constant digital notifications, students' academic trajectories could be derailed... Corrected the non-count noun usage of "evidences" to "empirical data" and updated vocabulary selections to shift the text into an appropriate, authoritative academic register.
